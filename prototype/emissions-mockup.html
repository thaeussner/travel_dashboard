How the comparison is built

Flights only replace travel between the stops a flying traveller would also make. Overnight stops that exist only because of the overland route don't get their own flight unless you choose Every stop. Travel within a stop is the same in both worlds and cancels out.

Over time

Cumulative emissions of the route as travelled and of the flights that would have replaced it. The shaded bands are the 90 % intervals under the selected lens.

Journey by journey

Each row is one journey between stops in the current comparison: what it took on the ground and at sea, against the flight chain that would have replaced it. Log scale, so equal gaps mean equal ratios.

Reading this page

Switches vs. ranges

The switches are choices: equally defensible ways of counting that give different answers. They are never blended into an error bar. The ranges are uncertainty within one way of counting: factors, distances, engine loads and the size of aviation's non-CO₂ effect, sampled together 4,000 times.

The strip at the top shows the headline under all 36 combinations of switches, so you can see whether the conclusion depends on the method.

Fair comparison

Every switch applies to the actual trip and to the flights alike. Occupancy "as observed" can only be observed on vehicles you actually took. The flights you didn't take always use typical load factors.

The comparison holds the destinations fixed. It doesn't model a different trip you might have taken if you flew.
